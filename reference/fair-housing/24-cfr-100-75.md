**Read this before citing anything else about fair housing advertising.** Almost every
real estate advertising guide on the web cites **24 CFR Part 109**, the old Fair Housing
Advertising rule with the word lists and the Equal Housing Opportunity logo tables. **Part 109
was withdrawn from the Code of Federal Regulations by HUD directive FR-4029-F-01, effective
1 May 1996.** It has not been part of the CFR for thirty years. It is absent from every annual
CFR edition on govinfo that this project checked, 2010 through 2019, and requesting it from the
eCFR returns nothing. Part 100 is the live rule and this section is the operative one for
advertising.

Paragraph (d) below still cross-references Part 109. **That dead cross-reference is in the
current official text**, which is a large part of why the mistake keeps propagating: HUD's own
live regulation points at a part HUD removed in 1996. It is reproduced here unaltered, because
this file is verbatim and the auditor does not silently correct a regulator.

The rescinded word list is vendored separately at
[`24-cfr-109-20-rescinded.md`](24-cfr-109-20-rescinded.md), clearly labelled, for stage 11's
flags only. **It can never produce a finding.**

**Consequence of this being the live rule:** the Equal Housing Opportunity logo is **not a
current federal requirement**. It was a "should" in a rescinded part. This auditor therefore
does **not** issue a finding for a missing logo. State law, a brokerage, or an MLS may still
require it, and that is out of scope here.

**Provision ID scheme.** `FHA-100.75(a)`, `FHA-100.75(b)`, `FHA-100.75(c)(1)` through `(c)(4)`.

## The section, verbatim

### § 100.75 Discriminatory advertisements, statements and notices.

**(a)** It shall be unlawful to make, print or publish, or cause to be made, printed or published, any notice, statement or advertisement with respect to the sale or rental of a dwelling which indicates any preference, limitation or discrimination because of race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national origin, or an intention to make any such preference, limitation or discrimination.

**(b)** The prohibitions in this section shall apply to all written or oral notices or statements by a person engaged in the sale or rental of a dwelling. Written notices and statements include any applications, flyers, brochures, deeds, signs, banners, posters, billboards or any documents used with respect to the sale or rental of a dwelling.

**(c)** Discriminatory notices, statements and advertisements include, but are not limited to:

**(1)** Using words, phrases, photographs, illustrations, symbols or forms which convey that dwellings are available or not available to a particular group of persons because of race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national origin.

**(2)** Expressing to agents, brokers, employees, prospective sellers or renters or any other persons a preference for or limitation on any purchaser or renter because of race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national origin of such persons.

**(3)** Selecting media or locations for advertising the sale or rental of dwellings which deny particular segments of the housing market information about housing opportunities because of race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national origin.

**(4)** Refusing to publish advertising for the sale or rental of dwellings or requiring different charges or terms for such advertising because of race, color, religion, sex, handicap, familial status, or national origin.

**(d)** 24 CFR part 109 provides information to assist persons to advertise dwellings in a nondiscriminatory manner and describes the matters the Department will review in evaluating compliance with the Fair Housing Act and in investigating complaints alleging discriminatory housing practices involving advertising.

## How this auditor uses it, and its hard limit

`FHA-100.75` is a **judgement provision**, not a mechanical one. Whether a phrase "indicates a
preference" is exactly the kind of call a person must make, and getting it wrong in either
direction is expensive: a false accusation of housing discrimination is defamatory, and a
missed one leaves real exposure.

So the rule in `rules.md` section 6 applies at its strictest here. This auditor:

- **Lists** every phrase on the page that names or implies a protected class, or a proxy for
  one, with its line number, as REQUIRES HUMAN.
- **Never issues a FAIL** against `FHA-100.75` from source text alone.
- **Never clears a page either.** The verdict is REQUIRES HUMAN, not PASS, because absence of
  an obvious phrase is not evidence of compliance.

The value is the located list, handed to a person who can judge it. That is what an auditor
owes here, and it is the honest limit of what a file reader can do.
