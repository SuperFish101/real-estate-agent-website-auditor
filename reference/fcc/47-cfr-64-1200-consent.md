**Why a website audit cares.** An agent's site captures a phone number, and the agent then
texts that lead. If those texts are marketing sent with an autodialer, the consent has to have
been captured **at the form**, in writing, with specific disclosures. The form is on the
website, so the compliance artifact is on the website.

**A caution recorded in the file, because the rule moved recently.** The FCC's 2023 order
modified this definition. The Eleventh Circuit vacated that change, and the FCC reverted the
rule to the earlier definition. The text below is the current eCFR text. Re-check the source
before relying on it, and treat any commentary written between 2023 and 2025 as suspect.

**Provision ID scheme.** `TCPA-64.1200(f)(9)`.

---

## The definition, verbatim

**(9)** The term *prior express written consent* means an agreement, in writing, bearing the signature of the person called that clearly authorizes the seller to deliver or cause to be delivered to the person called advertisements or telemarketing messages using an automatic telephone dialing system or an artificial or prerecorded voice, and the telephone number to which the signatory authorizes such advertisements or telemarketing messages to be delivered.

**(i)** The written agreement shall include a clear and conspicuous disclosure informing the person signing that:

**(A)** By executing the agreement, such person authorizes the seller to deliver or cause to be delivered to the signatory telemarketing calls using an automatic telephone dialing system or an artificial or prerecorded voice; and

**(B)** The person is not required to sign the agreement (directly or indirectly), or agree to enter into such an agreement as a condition of purchasing any property, goods, or services.

Related definition, from the same section:

**(3)** The term *clear and conspicuous* means a notice that would be apparent to the reasonable consumer, separate and distinguishable from the advertising copy or other disclosures.

## Provision index

| ID | Obligation | How it is checked on a form |
|---|---|---|
| `TCPA-64.1200(f)(9)-SCOPE` | The rule bites only where marketing calls or texts are sent with an autodialer or prerecorded voice | Does the form capture a telephone number at all? If not, NOT APPLICABLE. Whether the agent then autodials is REQUIRES HUMAN, and the auditor asks rather than assumes |
| `TCPA-64.1200(f)(9)-AGREEMENT` | An agreement in writing, bearing a signature, that clearly authorizes the seller | Is there consent text at the point of capture, tied to a deliberate act such as a checkbox? A pre-ticked box is not a signature |
| `TCPA-64.1200(f)(9)(i)(A)` | Disclosure that signing authorizes autodialed or prerecorded telemarketing | Does the consent text say so, in those terms? |
| `TCPA-64.1200(f)(9)(i)(B)` | Disclosure that signing is **not a condition of purchasing** any property, goods or services | Is that sentence present? This is the one almost always missing |
| `TCPA-64.1200(f)(3)` | The disclosure must be "clear and conspicuous", meaning "separate and distinguishable" from other copy | Is the consent text its own element, or buried inside a terms link? Whether a reasonable consumer would notice it is REQUIRES HUMAN |

## Severity

Graded **LEGAL**. The TCPA carries a private right of action with statutory damages per
message, which is why it is the most litigated rule on this list. The auditor reports the
absence of required disclosure text at the point of capture. It does not and cannot establish
whether the agent actually sends autodialed marketing, so a finding always names that
assumption.
