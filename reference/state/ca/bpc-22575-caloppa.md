**Why this applies to essentially every real estate agent's website.** The trigger is
collecting "personally identifiable information through the Internet about individual
consumers residing in California." A lead capture form, a home valuation form, a newsletter
signup or a contact form all collect it. There is no small business exemption.

**Provision ID scheme.** `CALOPPA-22575(a)`.

---

## Subdivision (a), verbatim

An operator of a commercial Web site or online service that collects personally identifiable information through the Internet about individual consumers residing in California who use or visit its commercial Web site or online service shall conspicuously post its privacy policy on its Web site, or in the case of an operator of an online service, make that policy available in accordance with paragraph (5) of subdivision (b) of Section 22577. An operator shall be in violation of this subdivision only if the operator fails to post its policy within 30 days after being notified of noncompliance.

## Subdivision (b), what the policy must contain

Not reproduced here. Subdivision (b) requires the policy to identify the categories of
personally identifiable information collected and the categories of third parties with whom
it may be shared, describe the process for a consumer to review and request changes to their
information, describe how the operator notifies consumers of material changes, identify its
effective date, disclose how the operator responds to Do Not Track signals, and disclose
whether third parties may collect PII about the consumer's activity across sites over time.

Read the full text at the source URL before making any finding about the policy's **content**.
This auditor makes findings only about whether a policy is **posted and conspicuous**.

## Severity and the thirty day rule

Graded **LEGAL**, with one honest qualifier that must appear in every finding: the statute
says an operator "shall be in violation of this subdivision **only if** the operator fails to
post its policy **within 30 days after being notified** of noncompliance." So a missing policy
is not yet a violation. It is the condition that becomes one thirty days after somebody sends
a notice.

The auditor states it exactly that way. Saying "you are breaking the law" here would be
wrong, and saying nothing would be worse.
